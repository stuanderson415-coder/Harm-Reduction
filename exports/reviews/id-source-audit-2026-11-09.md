# ID profile source audit — 9 November 2026

Only reference fields were changed in the application. No dose, effect, timeline, risk, interaction, harm-reduction or emergency wording was edited. A semantic comparison of every non-reference field against the saved original confirms this.

## What the references mean

Links appear through the existing “Further information” display. They support the general subject and specific supported areas below; they are not blanket citations for every claim on a profile. No profile is certified fully correct by this audit. Original provenance of the legacy text remains unestablished. The classification model is not itself evidence for clinical content.

This is a source-supported editorial screening of all 15 profiles, not a systematic literature review or clinical sign-off. Missing evidence means “not verified in the sources checked”, not “proven false”. Approximate timelines can differ legitimately by formulation, route, study and population. No universal safe dose is established by these sources.

## Highest priority before publication

1. Nitrous oxide: remove the 30-second emergency-call delay.
2. GHB: remove dose volumes without concentration and the fixed GBL conversion.
3. Ketamine, GHB, opioids and benzodiazepines: distinguish recovery position (unconscious but breathing normally) from CPR (unresponsive and not breathing normally).
4. Mushrooms: remove the promise effects will wear off safely; identity uncertainty requires poisoning advice.
5. Cocaine: remove the claim that testing verifies absence of contaminants and correct the cocaethylene multiplier.
6. Cannabis: remove CHS “only hot showers” and address conflicting edible dose/redosing guidance.
7. Review all universal dose bands, precise peaks and absolute statements before treating the ID sheets as ready for public health use.

These corrections are proposals only, pending user review.

## Scope and remaining work

Core effects and risks were compared with the listed sources; specialised quantitative, molecular, local-frequency and subjective claims remain flagged where not individually established. Street names, every possible appearance and every rare outcome were not independently validated. A clinician/pharmacist should review proposed emergency and medication wording before claiming clinical validation. No changes have been published.
