# Substance combination review — 9 November 2026

Review only: app classifications have not been changed.

D = Dangerous; U = Unsafe; C = Caution; N = Not assessed / insufficient pair-specific evidence. These are proposed editorial labels for this app, not a validated clinical scale. A source link may support the described harm or class-level concern without assigning that label. N does not mean safe.

All combinations carry risk. Dose, contents/purity, route, tolerance, health, medication, setting, emotional state and time between substances matter.

## Corrections needed before publication

- Only 18 of the 66 selectable pairs currently have reachable explicit records; 48 use the generic fallback. One further record targets an unavailable “psychedelics” identifier.
- Missing pairs must not automatically be called Caution. Use Not assessed / insufficient information.
- Remove blanket “reduce both doses by at least 50%”, timed peak-alignment instructions, and unconditional “physiologically safe” claims. They do not establish a safe regimen.
- Replace mechanism claims that exceed evidence: “complete arrest”, “exponential”, “most common trigger”, unsupported population percentages, and fixed duration assumptions across all opioids.
- Cocaethylene's reported 18–25-fold association concerns selected sudden-death evidence, not a universal multiplier of cardiotoxicity for every person or dose.
- No green rating is proposed from this review. That is an evidence/presentation choice, not proof that all remaining combinations are equally harmful.
- Cannabis + LSD has an ADF rare-psychosis warning. Cannabis + psychedelics can intensify difficult experiences, especially at higher cannabis doses in a prospective self-report study. That study cannot establish causation or quantify psychosis risk.
- MDMA + methamphetamine merits an explicit Unsafe warning: possible severe stimulant toxicity, heart strain, overheating and agitation. Severe outcomes can be life-threatening regardless of colour label.

## Evidence limits

This is a public-health-source audit, not a systematic review or clinical sign-off. ADF guidance is grounded in its cited literature but is not a validated numerical interaction scale. Pair-specific data are often sparse and class-level recommendations are marked through cautious wording. Review by an AOD clinician/pharmacist is required before describing this checker as clinically validated. The distinction between panic, perceptual changes and psychosis should be retained.
